\begin{abstract}
CUE descriptions specify possible completions of shared subjects. This proposal
extends that interpretation with higher-rank impredicative universal
quantification: one subject satisfies a predicate at every semantic type
instance. Function types are universal predicates on successful application,
with contravariant inputs and covariant results. Certification establishes these
predicates for open implementations using proved bounds on live references.
Evaluation and certification jointly propagate information through a scoped
constraint graph; exact residuals preserve their dependencies under subsequent
unification. A relevant source discipline checks the observations explicitly
claimed by interfaces and distinguishes persistent refutation from pending
obligations. The development establishes substitution, conservativity, semantic
soundness, progress and preservation for partial computations, and conditional
failure-free composition. A finite mandatory checking kernel, explicit proof
support, and accounted work limits specify how these guarantees are obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
CUE treats data and schemas as descriptions ordered by refinement. A declaration
constrains the possible completions of a subject; another declaration about the
same subject contributes another constraint. Unification is their conjunction.
This interpretation gives configuration composition its central invariant:
additional information restricts a result rather than replacing it.

Polymorphic interfaces express an obligation of a different logical shape.
An ordinary field may be completed by a suitable value. A universally quantified
field must admit one value that satisfies every instance of its description.
Applying this distinction to operational closures yields function contracts:
one implementation must respect the stated input--output relation at every
admissible input and every type instance. Universal quantification can therefore
supply higher-order interfaces while conjunction keeps its meaning as the
intersection of predicates on a shared subject.

The surrounding configuration remains open. A type expression can refer to a
field whose description is refined later, and a function body can capture a
value that is only partially known. A useful certificate must consequently
state its assumptions about those references. An established upper bound can
justify an operation before its operands are concrete, while the original
references continue to determine the operation's constraints. Further
unification strengthens the assumptions, supplies new proof premises, and
creates any additional obligations imposed by the new declarations.

This leads to a single organization of checking and execution. The source graph
retains exact constraints and their scopes. Finite certificates justify facts
about that graph and the behavior of its implementations. Evaluation can compute
premises needed by certification; certification can enable further evaluation.
Each step preserves the source relation, and unfinished work remains an explicit
residual. The same organization supports separately checked components, live
function interfaces, and later unification of evaluated source.

The semantic guarantee is partial correctness: successful returns satisfy their
contracts. The source discipline additionally checks declared capabilities and
explicitly stated observation regions. Operational safety excludes unsupported
eliminations, and composition preserves success when its components and call
constraints satisfy the corresponding success premises. These distinct
judgments give precise content to a certified configuration without identifying
an unresolved obligation with a failed computation.

\section{Semantic foundations}
\label{sec:semantics}

\subsection{Descriptions and joint completions}
Let $D$ be the set of complete data of the base language. A description denotes
a subset of $D$; a literal denotes a singleton. Semantic refinement is inclusion:
$A\le B$ means $\sem A\subseteq\sem B$. The interpretations of bottom, top,
unification, and disjunction are
\[
 \sem\bot=\varnothing,\qquad \sem\top=D,\qquad
 \sem{A\land B}=\sem A\cap\sem B,\qquad
 \sem{A\lor B}=\sem A\cup\sem B.
\]
These correspond to \code{_|_}, \code{_}, \code{&}, and \code{|}, with the
orientation of CUE's subsumption order~\cite{cuespec}. Semantic types are
\emph{extensional}: equality depends on their inhabitants.

An open description has a relational interpretation. With exposed coordinates
$E$ and local coordinates $L$, write
$P\subseteq\Env(E)\times\Env(L)$ for its joint completions. Local hiding is
existential projection:
\[
 \operatorname{hide}_L(P)=\{w\mid\exists\xi.\ P(w,\xi)\}.
\]
Thus ordinary values are existential in the following precise sense: completing
a declaration chooses witnesses satisfying its constraints. A reference can
share a witness, whereas copying a schema creates fresh cells with rebinding of
its internal references~\cite{cuerefs}. The distinction is expressed by
\[
 \exists x.\bigl(P(x)\land Q(x)\bigr)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (\exists x.P(x))\land(\exists y.Q(y)).
\]
The former uses one completion; the latter permits two. Consequently, fields
and their dependencies are combined before local witnesses are hidden.

A record predicate specifies field contents together with a presence policy.
An open record permits additional fields; an exact record fixes its allowed
labels. An optional field permits absence. Empty optional contents therefore
force absence, whereas empty required contents refute the record. Field
provenance, lexical scope, and the preference information carried by defaults
belong to the elaborated description and its observation policy. The predicate
of completions is its refinement component; preference selection follows the
base rules~\cite{cuedefaults}.

\subsection{Operational subjects and live references}
Fix a set $V$ extending $D$ with complete finite records, lists, argument
packets, and represented closures. A packet records ordered arguments, labeled
arguments, and omission information. A complete closure is represented by
$(o,\gamma,\beta)$, where $o$ is a stable code origin, $\gamma$ its free captured
environment, and $\beta$ its normalized bindings of partially supplied slots.
The code and captures determine its calling protocol and executable defaults.
Let $F\subseteq V$ be the closure inhabitants, and set $\U=\Pow(V)$.
The sort $\Type$ classifies semantic predicates in $\U$.
Write $\Num$, $\Str$, and $\Bool$ for the numeric, string, and Boolean
predicates.

Closure identity is equality of these operational descriptions. Copying,
embedding, and unrelated record refinement preserve the code origin and free
captures. Distinct captures distinguish closures produced by the same code.
The representations are finite; equality of complete descriptions is
structural. Symbolic capture equality is retained as a constraint on their
completions. This gives extensional predicates on inhabitants with intensional
identity.

An exposed interface $E$ includes both data coordinates and live description
coordinates. Its assignments form a set $\mathcal W_E$ of \emph{worlds}, subject
to the graph's lexical scopes and reference interpretation. A data coordinate
is a completion witness. A description coordinate is a predicate constraining
such witnesses. Source fields may name, compute, and refine these predicates.
A scoped reference $r$ retains its source node and its copy/rebinding map; write
$\tau_r$ for the type expression denoting that reference.

At a world $w$, and a valuation $\rho$ of type variables, the reference denotes
$\sem{\tau_r}_{w,\rho}\subseteq V$. For example, a live field bounded by
\code{int} imposes $\sem{\tau_r}_{w,\rho}\subseteq\Int$, where
$\Int\subseteq V$ is the integer predicate. A lexical alias of \code{int}
denotes the fixed predicate $\Int$. A successful singleton field fixes its
description to that singleton. Computed descriptions retain the equations and
operand demands that determine them. This interpretation keeps first-class
descriptions and their future refinement in the same scoped graph.

\begin{assumption}[Base semantic interface]\label{ass:base}
The base elaboration assigns each scoped graph its predicate of successful
joint completions. Source conjunction is intersection after scope alignment.
Reference interpretation commutes with capture-avoiding substitution and
pullback along context extension. Fresh local witnesses are hidden after
sharing is established. Legacy constructors, reference rules, and preference
selection retain their base interpretations.
\end{assumption}

Adding a declaration restricts this predicate on a common interface. The
interpretation of an existing reference is fixed; its admissible worlds become
more constrained. Observation may project away description coordinates, while
source intended for further unification retains the coordinates that later
declarations may address.

Operational application is fixed on represented closures independently of the
valuation of erased type variables. Write $f\,p\Downarrow v$ for a successful
return on a complete packet. A symbolic implementation selects a completed
closure $f_w$ in each world. Its data captures and represented assertions are
interpreted in that world. All subsequent semantic equations are pointwise in
$w$; the subscript is suppressed where no live reference occurs.

\subsection{Type expressions}
Types are finite expressions generated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grammar}
\begin{split}
 T,U ::= {}&\bot\mid\top\mid\alpha\mid\tau_r\mid k\mid c
          \mid T\land U\mid T\lor U\mid T\to U\\
          &\mid\{\ell_i^{m_i}:T_i\}_{i\in I}
          \mid[T_1,\ldots,T_n]\mid\List(T)
          \mid\forall\alpha\le U.\,T .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $k$ is a base kind or a represented closed first-order data predicate,
$c$ is a literal, and $m_i$ records field presence. A record also carries its
open or exact label policy. Acyclic aliases expand by capture-avoiding
substitution. The unbounded form $\forall\alpha.T$ abbreviates
$\forall\alpha\le\top.T$. A bound may use earlier type variables; its binder
scopes over the body only.

A function interface is formed in its enclosing graph and type-variable scope.
Its term parameters bind the function body. Scope checking follows references
transitively, including aliases and rebinding maps. The same rule applies to a
written type and to a live description used in its place.

Base predicates have their fixed interpretations, $\sem\alpha_{w,\rho}=
\rho(\alpha)$, and the lattice operations denote intersection and union in
$\U$. Records and tuples impose their component predicates.
$\List(A)$ contains the finite lists with every element in $A$, including the
empty list. Bottom is the empty predicate. Universal and arrow types receive
the interpretations below.

\subsection{Quantifiers on a fixed subject}
For a context set $X$, predicates form the inclusion-ordered fiber $\Pow(X)$.
A context map $h:Y\to X$ acts by inverse image $h^*P=h^{-1}(P)$.
For a projection $\pi:X\times I\to X$, define
\begin{align}
 \exists_\pi P&=\{x\mid\exists a\in I.\ (x,a)\in P\},
       \label{eq:exists}\\
 \forall_\pi P&=\{x\mid\forall a\in I.\ (x,a)\in P\}.
       \label{eq:forall}
\end{align}
The subject being described belongs to $X$. Quantification removes the bound
coordinate while keeping that subject fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Adjunction and reindexing]\label{prop:adjunction}
For $P\subseteq X\times I$ and $Q\subseteq X$,
\[
 \exists_\pi P\subseteq Q\iff P\subseteq\pi^*Q,
 \qquad
 \pi^*Q\subseteq P\iff Q\subseteq\forall_\pi P.
\]
For $h:Y\to X$, $h'=h\times\mathrm{id}_I$, and the corresponding projection
$\pi'$, both quantifiers obey
$h^*\mathcal Q_\pi P=\mathcal Q_{\pi'}h'^*P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first equivalence asserts that the first coordinate of every member of $P$
lies in $Q$. The second asserts that each $x\in Q$ has every $(x,a)$ in $P$.
At $y\in Y$, either side of the reindexing equation quantifies $P(h(y),a)$
over exactly $I$.
\end{proof}

Thus $\exists_\pi\dashv\pi^*\dashv\forall_\pi$. The subset hyperdoctrine
organizes both operations~\cite{lawvere,mellies}: the existential projection
used for ordinary completion has universal projection as its right-adjoint
dual. In each Boolean fiber,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duality}
 \forall_\pi P=\neg\exists_\pi\neg P.
\end{equation}
At a semantic type index, existential quantification is the union of the
instance predicates and universal quantification is their intersection.
Both quantify over the same carrier with the same substitution operation.

For a bound $U$, let
$\operatorname{Sub}_{w,\rho}(U)=
 \{A\subseteq V\mid A\subseteq\sem U_{w,\rho}\}$.
The universal type interpretation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poly}
 \sem{\forall\alpha\le U.T}_{w,\rho}
 =\bigcap_{A\in\operatorname{Sub}_{w,\rho}(U)}
       \sem T_{w,\rho[\alpha:=A]}.
\end{equation}
This intersection is an element of $\U$ and is available as another type
instance. Quantification is therefore impredicative. Arbitrary placement of the
constructor in \eqref{eq:grammar} gives higher rank. Interpretation recurses on
finite syntax over the already fixed set $\U$; operational inhabitants supply
the realizers of the predicates~\cite{pistone}.



Every bound admits the empty predicate. Hence
\[
 \sem{\forall\alpha.\alpha}=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \sem{\forall\alpha.\List(\alpha)}=\{[\,]\}.
\]
Instantiation at $\varnothing$ proves the exclusions; the empty list belongs
to every list instance. More generally, $\exists f.\forall\alpha.P(f,\alpha)$
requires one uniform subject, whereas $\forall\alpha.\exists f.P(f,\alpha)$
permits a subject chosen separately at each instance.

\subsection{Function types and application}
For a computation $e$, write $e\in\Comp(B)$ when every successful return of
$e$ belongs to $B$. For a completed value, $v\in\Val(B)$ means $v\in B$.
Constraint failure, written $\Fail$, is an unsuccessful computation outcome.

\begin{definition}[Partial arrow]\label{def:arrow}
For predicates $A,B\subseteq V$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arrow}
 A\to B=\{f\in F\mid\forall p\in A.\ \forall v.\
                 f\,p\Downarrow v\Longrightarrow v\in B\}.
\end{equation}
A unary argument is identified with its unary packet.
\end{definition}

An arrow is thus a universal behavioral predicate on one operational subject.
Its result predicate is independent of the bound packet. In particular,
$\varnothing\to B=F$, and a closure that always fails belongs to
$A\to\varnothing$. The latter can contain closures even though
$\Val(\varnothing)$ is empty.

\begin{proposition}[Arrow algebra]\label{prop:arrows}
For all predicates $A,B,C,D$,
\begin{align*}
 A\subseteq C,\quad D\subseteq B
     &\Longrightarrow (C\to D)\subseteq(A\to B),\\
 (A\cup C)\to B&=(A\to B)\cap(C\to B),\\
 A\to(B\cap D)&=(A\to B)\cap(A\to D).
\end{align*}
When $\alpha$ is independent of $A$,
$A\to\sem{\forall\alpha.T}
 =\bigcap_{X\in\U}(A\to\sem T_{[\alpha:=X]})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first inclusion restricts the inputs tested and weakens the required result.
The second splits the input quantifier over a union; the third requires both
result predicates on each successful return. The final equation exchanges the
universal quantifiers over inputs, returns, and type instances.
\end{proof}

Input contravariance and result covariance follow from the interpretation.
Conjoined arrows retain their correlations: $(\Int\to\Str)\land
(\Str\to\Int)$ specifies two input regions of the same closure. The predicate
$\bigcap_{A\in\U}(A\to A)$ contains partial identities. Indeed, choosing
$A=\{x\}$ forces every successful return on $x$ to equal $x$.

An implementation constrained by a type has predicate $\{f\}\cap\sem T$.
For closure and packet sets define
\[
 \App(X,Q)=\{v\mid\exists f\in X,\ p\in Q.\ f\,p\Downarrow v\}.
\]
Sharing remains in the joint environment until projection.
\begin{proposition}[Application refinement]\label{prop:app}
$\App(X\cap Y,Q)\subseteq\App(X,Q)\cap\App(Y,Q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A closure and packet witnessing the left side witness both right-hand
memberships. The reverse inclusion can fail: distinct closures may return the
same value on a packet while their singleton predicates are disjoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Type substitution]\label{lem:substitution}
For every well-formed, capture-avoiding substitution, including its action on
reference graphs,
\[
 \sem{T[B/\alpha]}_{w,\rho}
 =\sem T_{w,\rho[\alpha:=\sem B_{w,\rho}]}.
\]
The instance $B$ may itself contain universal quantifiers.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $T$. The variable case is substitution into $\rho$; the reference
case is Assumption~\ref{ass:base}. Structural and lattice constructors preserve
the induction equations. At a fresh universal binder, the bound equation gives
the same indexing set and the body equation gives equal predicates at every
index. For arrows, substitution leaves the operational application relation
fixed and substitutes its two predicates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Quantifier algebra and refinement}
For a common range $I$, and a predicate $Q$ independent of $a$,
\begin{align}
 (\forall a\in I.P)\land(\forall a\in I.R)
       &=\forall a\in I.(P\land R),\label{eq:forallmeet}\\
 (\exists a\in I.P)\land Q
       &=\exists a\in I.(P\land Q),\label{eq:frobenius}\\
 (\forall a\in I.P)\land Q
       &=\forall a\in I.(P\land Q)\quad(I\ne\varnothing).
       \label{eq:floating}
\end{align}
Membership proves these identities. Type bounds always have nonempty ranges,
so the last identity applies to independent fields under a type binder.
Independent universals commute by exchanging their indices.

Bounds remain part of the predicate. In particular,
\[
 (\forall A\subseteq B.P(A))\land(\forall A\subseteq C.P(A))
 =\forall A\in\operatorname{Sub}(B)\cup\operatorname{Sub}(C).P(A).
\]
This range can be smaller than $\operatorname{Sub}(B\cup C)$: a mixed
integer--string predicate lies below neither of those disjoint kinds.
Shrinking a universal's range weakens its obligation, while conjoining another
predicate strengthens the constrained subject.

Universal projection preserves intersections. Its interaction with unions is
strictly different. With range $\{0,1\}$, take $P(a,z)\equiv z=a$ and
$Q(a,z)\equiv z=1-a$. Then $\forall a.(P\lor Q)=\{0,1\}$, while
$(\forall a.P)\lor(\forall a.Q)=\varnothing$. Projection can also lose
correlations: for $R_i=\{(s,i)\}$, first-coordinate projection $p$ gives
$p(R_0\cap R_1)=\varnothing$ but $p(R_0)\cap p(R_1)=\{s\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone refinement]\label{thm:monotonicity}
Let $P'\subseteq P$ on a common scoped context. Any composite $\mathcal Q$ of
reindexing and existential or universal projection over fixed ranges satisfies
$\mathcal Q(P')\subseteq\mathcal Q(P)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inverse image preserves inclusion. An existential witness for $P'$ is a
witness for $P$; satisfaction of all $P'$-instances implies satisfaction of all
$P$-instances. Composition of these monotone maps preserves inclusion.
\end{proof}

Live bounds fit this theorem by retaining their coordinates. A bounded
universal is the fixed formula
\[
 \forall A\in\U.\
    \bigl(A\subseteq\sem U_{w,\rho}\Longrightarrow P(w,\rho,A)\bigr).
\]
Refining the enclosing graph restricts $w$ in this formula. An established empty
observation remains empty; a singleton observation can retain its atom or
become empty.

\begin{theorem}[Data-relative conservativity]\label{thm:conservative}
Let $i:D\hookrightarrow V$ be the data embedding. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:base}, every legacy description $T$ in a legacy environment $\eta$ satisfies
\[
 i^{-1}(\sem T^+_{i\eta})=\sem T^0_\eta.
\]
Using the base evaluation and observation rules on legacy syntax preserves
legacy data observations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on legacy elaboration. The base predicates restrict to their original
sets, and inverse image preserves intersection and union. Data constructors
and copied references preserve the embedding and sharing. Local hiding uses
the original data witnesses. The unchanged base evaluation and observation
rules give the same data and preference selections by induction on their
derivations.
\end{proof}

Global top ranges over $V$, and its data restriction is the original top.
The extension is consequently conservative at the data interface while
providing additional operational subjects and predicates on them.

\section{Certification of open descriptions}
\label{sec:typing}
The semantic interpretation specifies which inhabitants satisfy an interface.
Certification supplies finite evidence that a particular implementation does
so. For an open implementation the statement is relative to its surrounding
constraints, and its evidence retains the references on which the statement
depends.

\subsection{Stores, bounds, and proof support}
A \emph{store} $S\subseteq\mathcal W_E$ is a predicate on a fixed exposed
interface. Write $S'\sqsubseteq S$ for $S'\subseteq S$, and
$S\models\phi$ for $\forall w\in S.\phi(w)$. A context extension first
pulls the store back along the map forgetting its fresh coordinates. Store
comparisons are then made on the common context.

A rigid type context is an ordered list
$\Delta=\alpha_1\le U_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\le U_n$, with each bound using
earlier variables only. Write $\rho\models_w\Delta$ for satisfaction of
these inclusions at $w$. A fact certificate
\[
 S;\Delta\vdash\phi
\]
proves $\forall w\in S\,\forall\rho\models_w\Delta.\phi(w,\rho)$.
A certificate is a finite proof graph checked by a kernel. Its leaves are
kernel axioms, independently available source facts, or explicitly named
hypotheses; its inference dependencies are well founded. An open hypothesis
remains displayed until a rule discharges it or linking supplies its evidence.

A goal has its own assumption store. The full source relation contains the
interface being required; a proof of that interface uses the independently
justified premises recorded in its support. In particular, an implementation's
result annotation is a proof goal. Using a callback's contract under a named
parameter hypothesis is justified by later certified substitution. These two
roles of a contract are determined by its proof scope.

For a reference $r$, upper and lower bounds are certificates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
 S;\Delta\vdash\tau_r\preceq U_r,
 \qquad
 S;\Delta\vdash L_r\preceq\tau_r,
\end{equation}
where $\preceq$ in a certificate denotes pointwise semantic inclusion.
Intersecting established upper bounds and joining established lower bounds
improves precision. Relational facts among several references remain available
alongside these summaries. For closed summaries, the optimal bounds are
\[
 U^*_S(r)=\bigcup_{\substack{w\in S\\\rho\models_w\Delta}}
                 \sem{\tau_r}_{w,\rho},\qquad
 L^*_S(r)=\bigcap_{\substack{w\in S\\\rho\models_w\Delta}}
                 \sem{\tau_r}_{w,\rho}.
\]
A computed pair is sound when $L_r\subseteq L^*_S(r)$ and
$U^*_S(r)\subseteq U_r$. These equations characterize precision; the checker
manipulates represented bounds and their proofs. Empty unions and intersections
have values $\varnothing$ and $V$, respectively. An independently refuted store
is reported as inconsistent.

\begin{lemma}[Persistence]\label{lem:persistence}
If $S;\Delta\vdash\phi$ and $S'\sqsubseteq S$, then the same proof with a
weakened store premise establishes $S';\Delta\vdash\phi$. Moreover,
$U^*_{S'}(r)\subseteq U^*_S(r)$ and $L^*_S(r)\subseteq L^*_{S'}(r)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every world and rigid valuation admitted by the stronger store is admitted by
the original one. Restricting the indices of a universal assertion preserves
it; restricting a union shrinks it and restricting an intersection enlarges it.
\end{proof}

The target expression, scope, and rebinding map are fixed in this lemma.
A proved upper bound on $X$ licenses its use in the proved direction. For
example, $X\preceq\Int$ permits an $X$-argument to be used arithmetically;
membership of a particular integer in $X$ requires the opposite-direction
fact. In an arrow domain the inclusion reverses:
$\Int\to B\preceq X\to B$. Each approximation of a compound expression
therefore carries a proof for that expression.

The \emph{support} $\supp(q)$ of a judgment is the transitive closure of its
free graph references, lexical binders, proof hypotheses, and protocol
dependencies. Substitution, freshness checks, and proof-cache keys use this
support. A cached theorem is reusable by persistence. A result conditional on
a temporary equality retains that equality as a premise, and a search status
is revisited when its dependencies change.

\subsection{Values, computations, and elimination evidence}
Let $\Gamma=x_1:T_1,\ldots,x_n:T_n$ record hypotheses about values available
on successful completion. The two core judgments are
\[
 \jud\vdash_v v:T,
 \qquad
 \jud\vdash e:\Comp(T).
\]
A source data reference contributes its established bound together with a
\emph{validation demand}: the operand must satisfy all its retained source
constraints when demanded. A function reference contributes invocation evidence
or an explicit hypothesis to be discharged at linking. This distinction allows
a body to be checked using a partial capture while retaining the work required
to obtain its value.

Description lookup is distinct from materialization. A required declaration
\code{z!: int} establishes an integer bound in every successful completion
without supplying a value for \code{z}. Both a lexical reference and a field
selection may use that bound in a body proof. Neither operation discharges the
runtime presence and validation demands. An optional declaration alone does
not establish that its field is available.

The core contains variables, literals, closures, strict application and let,
records, tuples, selection, Boolean conditionals, meet, assertions, primitives,
and $\Fail$. It executes completed subjects of the source graph. In particular,
source unification of open records shares a common completion before the core
performs its completed-value check.
The source checker therefore collects the declarations of a record meet before
checking its fields. Internal references are rebound to that common subject;
external references retain their lexical coordinates. Named descriptions,
embeddings, and abbreviations preserve this rule. Collection retains required
and optional presence, definition boundaries, and source observations. A
description's current fields do not by themselves establish an exact inventory.
In particular, checking a method before collecting its sibling refinements
would demand a theorem about an operand that the source never executes alone.

\begin{assumption}[Primitive and completion interface]\label{ass:operations}
A primitive supplies a packet interface, a successful-result interface, and
finite transport of their evidence. On complete certified operands it returns a
value, fails, or takes a primitive step. Every such step preserves the result
certificate. Source completion and core execution simulate one another on
successful data observations, retaining the source's packet and validation
demands. Failure retains its branch and scope.
\end{assumption}

Proof decorations may record type abstraction $\Lambda\alpha.e$ and
instantiation $e[T]$. Their \emph{erasure} removes the decorations:
$\erase{\Lambda\alpha.e}=\erase e$ and $\erase{e[T]}=\erase e$.
It retains code, captures, protocols, defaults, and executable assertions.
Thus $\erase{e\mathbin{\&}P}=\erase e\mathbin{\&}P$ for an assertion.

The main rules are as follows. A missing type-context prefix in a premise is
the common $S;\Delta$.
\begin{mathpar}
 \inferrule*[right=Var]{x:T\in\Gamma}{\jud\vdash_v x:T}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Return]{\jud\vdash_v v:T}
   {\jud\vdash v:\Comp(T)}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Abs]{S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:A\vdash e:\Comp(B)}
   {\jud\vdash_v\lambda x.e:A\to B}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=App]{\jud\vdash f:\Comp(A\to B)\\
                       \jud\vdash a:\Comp(A)}
   {\jud\vdash f\,a:\Comp(B)}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Let]{\jud\vdash e:\Comp(A)\\
                       S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:A\vdash d:\Comp(B)}
   {\jud\vdash\mathsf{let}\ x=e\ \mathsf{in}\ d:\Comp(B)}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Fail]{~}{\jud\vdash\Fail:\Comp(\bot)}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Meet]{\jud\vdash e:\Comp(A)\\
                        \jud\vdash d:\Comp(B)}
   {\jud\vdash e\land d:\Comp(A\land B)}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=Sub]{\jud\vdash e:\Comp(A)\\S;\Delta\vdash A\preceq B}
   {\jud\vdash e:\Comp(B)}.
\end{mathpar}
Value subsumption has the corresponding form. Literals synthesize singletons.
A record or tuple constructor combines its component certificates; selection
requires a guaranteed field or a certified tuple coordinate. General list
indexing is a checked primitive. Conditionals require a Boolean condition and
certificates for both branches. A branch may use facts implied by its checked
condition, scoped to that branch. Primitive application uses the packet and
result evidence in Assumption~\ref{ass:operations}.

Both judgments admit intersection introduction for the same erased term and
union introduction. Write $\vdash_\star e:T$ for either judgment, with $T$
inside $\Comp$ in the computation case. Their universal rules are
\begin{mathpar}
 \inferrule*[right=All-I]{S;\Delta,\alpha\le U;\Gamma\vdash_\star e:T\\
                 \alpha\notin\supp(S,\Gamma,\erase e)}
   {\jud\vdash_\star e:\forall\alpha\le U.T}
 \and
 \inferrule*[right=All-E]{\jud\vdash_\star e:\forall\alpha\le U.T\\
              S;\Delta\vdash B:\Type\\S;\Delta\vdash B\preceq U}
   {\jud\vdash_\star e:T[B/\alpha]}.
\end{mathpar}
The variable $\alpha$ is fresh for $\Delta,U$. An instance is a finite scoped
type expression, including a quantified expression or a live reference.
Generalization retains one implementation independently of the rigid instance;
elimination records the chosen expression and its bound proof.

Union elimination uses one evaluated subject:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union-cut}
 \frac{\jud\vdash_v v:A\lor B\qquad
       S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:A\vdash d:\Comp(C)\qquad
       S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:B\vdash d:\Comp(C)}
      {\jud\vdash d[v/x]:\Comp(C)}.
\end{equation}
Here $x$ is fresh for $\Gamma,v$; the value version is analogous. A general
scrutinee is first named by strict let. Both branches certify the same
continuation. Thus the proof preserves the evaluated subject and its sharing,
as in value-restricted contextual union checking~\cite{unions}.

A \emph{checkable predicate} $P$ is a represented first-order constraint whose
forwarding test has the primitive contract: a successful test returns its
operand in $P$, and a failed test produces $\Fail$. Live operands retain their
validation demands. Its rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assertion}
 \frac{\jud\vdash e:\Comp(A)\qquad S;\Delta\vdash P\ \mathsf{checkable}}
      {\jud\vdash e\mathbin{\&}P:\Comp(A\land P)}.
\end{equation}
The assertion is executable. Its successful postcondition supplies the narrower
certificate; further conjuncts remain attached to the demanded predicate.

\subsection{Subtyping and implementation attachment}
Subtyping evidence includes reflexivity, explicit transitivity, bottom and top,
base-kind inclusions, literal membership, distributive-lattice rules, record
width/depth and presence, and covariant tuples and lists. Bound lookup gives
$\alpha\preceq U$ for $\alpha\le U\in\Delta$. Proved store facts supply
reference inclusions. The function and universal rules are
\begin{mathpar}
 \inferrule{S;\Delta\vdash A\preceq C\\S;\Delta\vdash D\preceq B}
   {S;\Delta\vdash(C\to D)\preceq(A\to B)}
 \and
 \inferrule{S;\Delta\vdash B\preceq U}
   {S;\Delta\vdash(\forall\alpha\le U.T)\preceq T[B/\alpha]}
 \and
 \inferrule{S;\Delta,\alpha\le U\vdash C\preceq T\\
            \alpha\notin\supp(S,\Delta,C)}
   {S;\Delta\vdash C\preceq(\forall\alpha\le U.T)}.
\end{mathpar}
Proposition~\ref{prop:arrows} supplies the arrow equalities. Each subtyping
rule transports value evidence: arrows retain an invocation derivation, records
retain component evidence, and unions retain arm evidence. This transport is
part of a core subsumption certificate. Purely semantic inclusion facts may
also be retained for reasoning about predicates.

An implementation comprises its origin, captures, body $e$, and declared packet
interface $C$. Attaching a requirement $A\to B$ creates the goals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:attachment}
 S;\Delta\vdash A\preceq C,
 \qquad
 S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:A\vdash e:\Comp(B).
\end{equation}
The first certifies the calling capability; the second certifies the body under
exactly the promised input and its certified captures. All accumulated
requirements are retained, together with their body derivations. A result
annotation supplies the second goal; an assertion supplies a test governed by
\eqref{eq:assertion}.

For a live input $X$ bounded by integers, the arithmetic primitive yields
\[
 S\vdash X\preceq\Int
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 S;\varnothing;x:X\vdash 2*x:\Comp(\Int).
\]
The implementation consequently has a certificate at $X\to\Int$ using only
that bound. Returning $x$ similarly establishes result $X$. Returning the
constant $2$ at result $X$ requires $\{2\}\preceq X$ or a retained assertion
that establishes membership on success. The input and result directions are
therefore determined by their proof obligations.

An invocation creates fresh packet cells and links a result cell to the body.
For the argument $2$ and the preceding arithmetic body, its source constraints
include
\begin{equation}\label{eq:livecall}
 p:\tau_r,\qquad p:2,\qquad a=2p,\qquad a:\Int.
\end{equation}
The packet references the live description according to its copy/rebinding map;
it does not write its argument back into the declaration. Later constraints
on the packet or result conjoin with this activation. Deriving $a=4$ preserves
the packet's validation demand, including when a body ignores an operand.
For a higher-order argument, linking also discharges the coverage goal in
\eqref{eq:attachment}. Narrowing a call packet and proving a universal
capability are distinct judgments.

\subsection{Relevant descriptors and explicit observations}
A partial arrow constrains successful returns. A source interface additionally
records which observations it explicitly claims. A \emph{relevant descriptor}
is a pair $\mathcal R=(T,\Omega)$, where $T$ is its semantic type and $\Omega$
a finite graph of explicit observations. The graph retains roots, arrow
clauses, type binders, alternatives, field-presence guards, and dependencies.
Its semantic erasure is $T$.

For a conjunctive arrow root $C=\bigwedge_{i\in I}(A_i\to B_i)$, define
\[
 D_C=\bigcup_i\sem{A_i},\qquad
 \mathcal B_C(p)=\bigcap_{i:p\in\sem{A_i}}\sem{B_i}\quad(p\in D_C).
\]
For each nonempty $J\subseteq I$, its exact input region and combined result are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regions}
 R_J=\left(\bigcap_{j\in J}\sem{A_j}\right)
       \setminus\left(\bigcup_{i\notin J}\sem{A_i}\right),
 \qquad
 B_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}\sem{B_j}.
\end{equation}
They are indexed by $w,\rho$. The inhabited $R_J$ partition the stated domain,
and $B_J$ is the obligation on its region. Complement is an internal operation
on these guards.

Observation traversal collects every conjunct of an explicit subject, descends
through written components and both arrow positions, and keeps alternatives
under their guards. Universals are examined under rigid variables. An
independent sibling domain may also determine a finite structural instance of
a universal clause: matching $\alpha\to\alpha$ against a sibling integer
domain anchors the instance $\Int\to\Int$. Repeated variables must match
identically, and the substitution and bound proofs must check. The dependency
graph records the sibling's independence through lexical transformations.

These are source observations. An inferred intermediate type or a call instance
retains the root of its declaration. Writing an instance as a separate interface
introduces a root for that interface. In particular, a generic result
$\alpha\land\beta$ is examined under rigid $\alpha,\beta$, while an
explicit result $\Int\land\Bool$ has a ground contradiction. Observation
origins remain through semantic absorption and simplification.

For a root in rigid context $\Delta$, define world predicates
\begin{align*}
 \Void_\Delta(B)(w)&\iff
       \forall\rho\models_w\Delta.\ B(w,\rho)=\varnothing,\\
 \Empty_\Delta(R)(w)&\iff
       \forall\rho\models_w\Delta.\ R(w,\rho)=\varnothing,\\
 \Reach_\Delta(R)(w)&\iff
       \exists\rho\models_w\Delta\,\exists p.\ p\in R(w,\rho).
\end{align*}
A checked proof of $\Void_\Delta(B_J)$ creates a demand that $R_J$ be empty.
An enclosing alternative or presence guard restricts the store of that demand.
The outcomes are determined by positive evidence:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}P{23mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
Outcome & Required evidence in the retained scope \\\midrule
Discharged & $S\models\Empty_\Delta(R_J)$. \\
Rejected & $S\models\Void_\Delta(B_J)$ and
           $S\models\Reach_\Delta(R_J)$. \\
Pending & The result has a uniform emptiness proof; the input question
          remains unresolved. \\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
A completed mandatory pass is \emph{quiet} at a root when it has discovered no
uniform result refutation. Quietness records completed search, and supplies
no logical premise. New bounds or evidence re-enqueue the root. A guarded
rejection becomes global only when its guard is required or all remaining
alternatives are rejected. Semantic emptiness of a guard discharges its local
demands; relevance rejection itself supplies no semantic emptiness fact.

\begin{definition}[Current relevant admission]\label{def:relevance}
For a finite checking kernel $K$, $\RC_K(S,\Delta,\Omega)$ holds when its
mandatory traversal is complete and every required root is discharged or quiet.
The admission record retains the observation graph, proof support, and triggers
for renewed checking.
\end{definition}

Thus $\Int\to\bot$ is rejected as an explicit interface even though it has
failure-only realizers. The generic result $\alpha\land\beta$ has no
uniform emptiness proof: $\alpha=\beta=\{0\}$ gives an inhabited instance.
The distinction is between a semantic type and its stated observations.

\begin{proposition}[Persistent rejection and observation coherence]
\label{prop:rc-coherence}
A checked rejection persists under store refinement in its retained scope.
Permutation, reassociation, duplication, alpha-renaming, and replacing
$A\to(B\land C)$ by $(A\to B)\land(A\to C)$ preserve observation questions
when dependencies and completed kernel work agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The rejection premises are positive facts about every admitted world;
Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence} preserves both. The listed transformations preserve
$\mathcal B_C(p)$ at every stated packet. Result splitting duplicates the
input guard and conjoins the same results. Canonical duplicate elimination and
scope-preserving renaming consequently identify the region and anchor graphs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Refuting an open goal}
Refutation of an open implementation must also respect its admitted worlds.
A sufficient successful-result countercertificate for $f:A\to B$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counterexample}
 \forall w\in S.\ \exists\rho\models_w\Delta,p,v.\quad
 p\in\sem A_{w,\rho}\ \land\ f_w p\Downarrow v\ \land\
 v\notin\sem B_{w,\rho}.
\end{equation}
The kernel verifies the execution and membership evidence. A witness may vary
with $w$; a fixed ground witness is a useful special case. At a closed universal
interface one admissible instance suffices. An analogous certificate exhibiting
a packet in $A\setminus C$ in every world refutes the coverage requirement of
\eqref{eq:attachment}. Coverage and relevant-admission refutations concern their
respective disciplines; \eqref{eq:counterexample} refutes the semantic contract.

A point in an upper approximation is only a possible witness. A live domain
bounded by $\Num$ can later become an integer domain, so a fractional point
in that upper approximation alone establishes no persistent violation. A fixed
numeric domain with an identity body and integer result does have the persistent
witness $1.5$. Failure and divergence supply no successful-result counterexample
to a partial arrow. Failed proof search leaves its goal pending.

\section{Evaluation by certified propagation}
\label{sec:checking}
Certification consumes facts about an open graph; evaluation can produce those
facts. Both processes therefore operate on the same retained source relation.
Their common invariant is exact residualization: each derived result carries
the constraints under which it was obtained.

\subsection{Exact residuals}
\label{sec:residual}
Let $G$ be a scoped graph and $P_G$ its predicate of successful joint completions
on an exposed interface $E$. The interface includes every coordinate available
to later source unification. After hiding only fresh implementation-local
nodes, a valid normalization $G\rightsquigarrow G'$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 P_{G'}=P_G\text{ on }E,
 \qquad
 P_{G'}\land R=P_G\land R
       \text{ for every future constraint }R\text{ on }E.
\end{equation}
The second equality follows from the first on this common interface. Its force
is the choice of interface: the retained graph includes live descriptions,
reference maps, binder scopes, closure origins, defaults, and validation
dependencies. It also retains the certification goals and source observations
needed to reconstruct the checking problem. Scalar projection alone loses
information needed by future source constraints.

A normalization can be justified conditionally. A \emph{guard} $H$ is a retained
predicate restricting the worlds in which its proof applies.
\begin{proposition}[Guarded normalization]\label{prop:guarded}
If $P=P'$ under $S\land H$, then
\[
 S\land P
 =S\land\bigl((H\land P')\lor(\neg H\land P)\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a world in $S$, the $H$ branch uses the assumed equivalence and the
complementary branch retains $P$. This proves equality at every world.
\end{proof}

A cache can represent the guarded result together with the original graph,
sharing the complementary residual. Its hypotheses remain part of its
support. Establishing a theorem under a proposed approximation therefore
records a conditional theorem; using it unconditionally requires a proof of
that approximation.

For the activation in \eqref{eq:livecall}, arithmetic may establish $a=4$.
The residual retains $p:\tau_r$ and the validation connecting $p$ to the
argument $2$. A later constraint excluding $2$ from $\tau_r$ still refutes the
call, and a later result constraint excluding $4$ still refutes its result.
The same requirement preserves demanded arguments of constant functions.

\subsection{States and transitions}
An evaluator state has the form
\[
 N=(G,\mathcal F,\mathcal C,\mathcal O,W).
\]
Here $G$ is the exact graph; $\mathcal F$ contains proved facts and bounds;
$\mathcal C$ contains core, inclusion, and protocol certificates;
$\mathcal O$ contains goals and observation triggers; and $W$ is the worklist.
Facts and certificates carry scope and proof support. Constraints accumulate
by conjunction, evidence by set union. Scheduling data have no semantic force.

The graph contains the semantic predicates of attached interfaces. A proof
uses its independently justified assumption store, as defined in
Section~\ref{sec:typing}. This separates conjunction of requirements from
establishment of the evidence needed to invoke an implementation.

The transition rules have five forms. \emph{Refinement} conjoins source
information and installs its scoped goals. \emph{Propagation} checks and records
a consequence of established premises. \emph{Certification} verifies a finite
derivation and its remaining hypotheses. \emph{Reduction} performs a justified
primitive or function step, preserving \eqref{eq:residual} and all operand
demands. Finally, \emph{refutation or suspension} records either checked
negative evidence or the unresolved obligation. Every new fact re-enqueues the
nodes depending on it.

An invocation is enabled by the required grounded core and protocol evidence
and a completed current admission pass. A symbolic reduction may expose useful
results while data demands remain. A \emph{derived atom} is such a summary with
its guards and validation dependencies. A \emph{completed value} has discharged
its current demanded validations and required certification obligations.
Export preserves its live source dependencies for subsequent refinement.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
propagate(state, budget):
    while work_remains(state) and budget_allows_step(budget):
        node = take_work(state)
        for item in one_accounted_step(node, state):
            verify_derivation_or_exact_rewrite(item)
            if accumulate_with_scope_and_support(state, item):
                enqueue_dependents(state, item)
    return retained_residual(state), scoped_diagnostics(state)
\end{lstlisting}
The dependency graph permits the cycle of useful work
\[
 \text{evaluation}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{stronger bounds}\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{certification}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{evaluation}.
\]
Accumulating partial information and notifying dependents follows the propagator
model~\cite{propagators}; the bounds are abstract summaries of the retained
concrete relation~\cite{abstract}.

\subsection{Grounding, suspension, and renewed checking}
Evidence dependencies are finite proof dependencies. A callable hypothesis used
to check a client is discharged by the actual implementation's certificate.
An implementation's pending annotation, and consequences borrowed from that
annotation, remain excluded from its own independent premise set. A cycle of
unproved goals therefore remains a cycle of residual obligations. An independent
data bound can establish a body theorem while the computation producing that
data remains cyclic or incomplete. Grounding a proof and completing its data
are separate conditions.

A residual obligation records its target, scope, dependencies, and reason for
waiting: missing data, missing evidence, a dependency cycle, or unfinished
budgeted work. Conditional reductions retain their hypotheses. A finite checked
inference may discharge hypotheses, including a supplied inductive proof;
membership in a dependency cycle supplies no such inference.

Under further source conjunction, existing theorems about fixed references
persist by Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence}. Additional requirements generate
additional goals. Current relevant admission and snapshot-dependent search
results are rechecked against their full dependency support. In this sense
recertification is the continued production of evidence for the live graph,
with theorem reuse justified by its explicit premises.

\subsection{The mandatory checking kernel}
A conforming kernel verifies the core rules, substitutions, instance bounds,
proof support, and protocol evidence. Its finite mandatory traversal includes
these direct checks on represented inputs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Base-kind disjointness, incompatible literals, finite literal unions,
and constant numeric intervals, including integer rounding at strict bounds;
ground tests use the enabled base primitives.
\item Required-field and presence conflicts, excluded exact-record fields,
and fixed tuple lengths and components. Impossible optional contents imply
absence, with the appropriate presence guard.
\item Ground primitive steps, invalid known indices, malformed argument order,
duplicate slot bindings, unknown labels, and missing required slots.
\item Guarded Boolean normalization. A union is semantically empty when every
alternative is refuted. A universal is empty when a checked admissible instance
is empty; instantiating $\forall\alpha.\alpha$ at $\bot$ is a direct case.
\item Aggregation of equal-domain arrow clauses, exact-region checks, and
verified structural anchors. The result check includes every clause active in
a region. Refuted results with unresolved input reachability create pending
demands.
\end{enumerate}
Ground, interval, record, and tuple witnesses supply direct reachability
certificates when their memberships hold throughout the current store.
For instance, $\{0,1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, and $\{0,2\}$ have inhabited pairwise
intersections and an empty triple intersection. Their three arrows over the
same fixed integer domain are jointly rejected by the mandatory region check.
Boolean decision graphs may share regions and proof subterms. Unfinished
mandatory work remains pending.

Additional proof search may be arbitrary; its accepted consequences are checked
by the kernel or by an extension satisfying the same soundness and evidence
transport conditions. A proved refutation, an unanswered goal, an inconclusive
search, and resource exhaustion are distinct outcomes. Only the first supplies
negative evidence.

\subsection{Work bounds and export}
Accounted work includes graph expansion, copying, matching, substitution,
normalization, integer bit operations, and proof verification. For an accounted
proof graph of $L$ nodes, each with local checking cost at most $C$, verification
uses $O(LC)$ time and $O(L)$ graph space, in addition to explicitly represented
payloads. Region generation and large instances are charged when constructed.
A fixed work allowance bounds the performed work and yields a residual at the
end of any finite run.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-basis saturation]\label{prop:saturation}
On a fixed finite fact-and-obligation basis, fair iteration of finitely many
monotone inflationary propagators reaches the least common fixed point above
its initial information. This fixed point is independent of scheduling. Every
finite prefix is sound when its local proof obligations are checked.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Only finitely many strict additions are possible. Fairness applies each
propagator after the last addition, so the resulting set is a common fixed
point. Monotonicity keeps every intermediate set below any common fixed point
above the initial one, proving leastness. Induction over checked additions
proves prefix soundness.
\end{proof}

Evaluation may generate new syntax and obligations. Equation~\eqref{eq:residual}
justifies every bounded prefix independently of saturation. Quiet admission
statuses are observations of completed checking work, rather than logical facts
in the propagation basis.

An exported source residual preserves the exposed interface, scope and origin
metadata, reference graph, validation demands, and observation roots. Proof
objects may be retained or reconstructed from their premises. Re-import first
reinstalls the obligations, merges additional constraints, and schedules their
affected nodes. A default-selected presentation retains the original alternatives
when intended for subsequent unification; comprehension expansion retains its
base observation dependencies. Thus source export preserves the refinement
problem as well as the currently displayed data.

\section{Soundness and compositionality}
\label{sec:metatheory}
The proof has two levels. Core certificates establish semantic membership and
safe elimination at each completed world. State invariants then lift those
results to partial evaluation of an open graph. The assumptions are the scoped
base interpretation in Assumption~\ref{ass:base}, the primitive and completion
interface in Assumption~\ref{ass:operations}, and exact symbolic rewrites as in
\eqref{eq:residual}.

\subsection{Substitution and semantic soundness}
\begin{lemma}[Certificate substitution]\label{lem:cert-subst}
A well-formed type substitution with evidence for its bound preserves core
certificates, including their stores and reference maps. If
$S;\Delta;\Gamma,x:A\vdash e:\Comp(B)$ and
$S;\Delta;\Gamma\vdash_v v:A$, then
$S;\Delta;\Gamma\vdash e[v/x]:\Comp(B)$. Value and simultaneous
substitution have the corresponding properties. Store refinement preserves
each certificate for its fixed target and scope.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the finite proof graph. Variable leaves use the supplied evidence;
fact leaves use their substituted proofs. Structural rules rebuild from the
induction hypotheses. Freshly rename a universal binder away from the
substitution's support; its generalization condition is then preserved. At
elimination use Lemma~\ref{lem:substitution} and composition of capture-avoiding
substitutions. Transform subtyping and transport proofs by the same induction.
For union elimination, rename its local variable $y$ and use
$(d[u/y])[v/x]=(d[v/x])[u[v/x]/y]$. Each discharged hypothesis is replaced
by its supplied derivation. Store weakening uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence} at every fact leaf. The source observation roots
keep their declaration scopes throughout this substitution.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Open certificate soundness]\label{thm:soundness}
Let $w\in S$, $\rho\models_w\Delta$, and let $\gamma$ supply values satisfying
$\Gamma$. If $\jud\vdash_v v:T$, its interpretation under $w,\rho,\gamma$
belongs to $\sem T_{w,\rho}$. If $\jud\vdash e:\Comp(T)$, every successful
return under that interpretation belongs to $\sem T_{w,\rho}$. A conditional
certificate has these conclusions whenever its displayed hypotheses hold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the certificate. Variables use the environment; literals use singleton
membership. Constructors and selections use their component predicates.
Abstraction extends $\gamma$ by an arbitrary member of the input predicate;
the body induction hypothesis proves the implication in \eqref{eq:arrow}.
Application first obtains an input member and then uses that implication.
Strict let substitutes its successful bound value. Failure has no successful
return. Meet restricts a shared completion to both predicates, and assertions
forward only passing operands.

Intersection introduction concerns the same execution. Union elimination
chooses the actual value's arm and uses the common continuation. Subtyping
rules establish inclusion, and primitives use Assumption~\ref{ass:operations}.
For universal introduction, its support condition permits varying the rigid
valuation without changing the store, environment, or erased computation.
Universal elimination uses Lemma~\ref{lem:substitution}. Fact leaves are valid
under their recorded premises, and well-founded proof dependencies close the
induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Relevant certification]\label{def:certified}
A current relevant certificate for $(T,\Omega)$ comprises a core certificate at
$T$, grounded attachment and protocol evidence, and
$\RC_K(S,\Delta,\Omega)$. It retains their supports and observation triggers.
For $w\in S$, let $\good_{S,K}(T,\Omega;w)$ be the completed values carrying
this evidence with their free hypotheses discharged.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Static refinement]\label{cor:static-refinement}
For every admissible rigid valuation,
\[
 \good_{S,K}(T,\Omega;w)\subseteq\sem T_{w,\rho}.
\]
This inclusion can be strict.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Erase the admission record and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} to the core
certificate. A failure-only closure inhabits $\Int\to\bot$, whose explicit
relevant root is rejected, witnessing strictness.
\end{proof}
Relevant certification refines semantic types as a source discipline. Its
admission component establishes neither termination nor a successful call.

\subsection{Safety of the completed core}
At a fixed world, substitute complete certified captures and operands. The
core uses left-to-right strict evaluation contexts for application, let,
constructors, selection, conditions, and primitive operands. Closure bodies
execute when invoked. Principal reductions include
\begin{align*}
 (\lambda x.e)\,v&\longrightarrow e[v/x],&
 \mathsf{let}\ x=v\ \mathsf{in}\ e&\longrightarrow e[v/x],\\
 \{\ldots,\ell=v,\ldots\}.\ell&\longrightarrow v,&
 [v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1}][i]&\longrightarrow v_i\quad(0\le i<n).
\end{align*}
A Boolean condition selects its branch. A completed assertion returns its
operand or fails. Completed meet returns the common subject when both
constraints hold, and otherwise fails. Source open-record unification has
already established the shared completion. Failure propagates through every
strict context, including a demanded argument unused by its function body.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical evidence]\label{lem:canonical}
A closed certified arrow value has closure and invocation evidence; a certified
guaranteed field is present; a fixed tuple has the certified length; and a
Boolean value is a Boolean literal. A nonfailure principal redex at a certified
elimination has a reduct with the same result certificate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on value evidence, including its transport derivations. Introductions
give their shapes. Arrow transport restricts the input premise and transports
the result. Intersection evidence retains both proofs: the common-domain arrow
law intersects their results, and the common-result law checks the same body
under both arms of a union input. Union evidence selects the value's actual
arm; quantified evidence substitutes its recorded instance. The resulting
invocation derivation justifies beta reduction by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cert-subst}. Field, tuple, and conditional reductions use their
component or branch proofs. Primitive reductions use
Assumption~\ref{ass:operations}. At an inhabited world,
Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness} excludes a certified value of bottom.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Core preservation and progress]\label{thm:preservation}
\label{thm:progress}
At a world satisfying the store and after certified substitution for free
values, reduction preserves a core computation certificate. A certified
computation is a value, is $\Fail$, or has a reduction step.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Canonical evidence and substitution preserve nonfailure principal reductions.
A failure reduct has certificate $\Comp(\bot)$ and subsumes to the original
result. Induction on strict contexts rebuilds the enclosing proof. Typing-only
intersection and universal rules rebuild for the same reduct; a value-restricted
union cut uses its selected arm evidence.

For progress, inspect the next demanded subexpression. Its step lifts and its
failure propagates. When it is a value, canonical evidence supplies the required
elimination shape, and the primitive contract supplies its next action.
Closures are values. Structural induction exhausts the core forms.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Preservation and progress of open states}
A state is \emph{well evidenced} when every fact and certificate has a valid
proof with its recorded support, every enabled invocation has its required
evidence, and each simplification retains exact source demands and obligations.

\begin{theorem}[State preservation]\label{thm:state-preservation}
The transitions of Section~\ref{sec:checking} preserve well-evidencedness.
Propagation, certification, and valid normalization preserve $P_G$. Adding an
external constraint $R$ gives $P_G\land R$ on the common interface and preserves
existing facts about fixed targets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Source refinement restricts the store and installs goals; persistence preserves
the existing premises. Propagation and certification check each added proof
with its support. Completed reductions use core preservation; symbolic
reductions use \eqref{eq:residual}. Refutation retains its evidence and scope,
and suspension changes no predicate. Induction over transitions proves the
invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Progress with residual obligations]\label{thm:state-progress}
For each demanded result, a well-evidenced state exposes a completed value, a
justified scoped failure or rejection, an enabled transition, or an exact
residual recording its unresolved obligations. Once an elimination's demanded
operands and required evidence are complete, it is not stuck for lack of a
supported elimination or callable protocol.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inspect the finite demand graph. A completed node has its validation and
certification evidence; a refuted node has its negative proof. At a nonterminal
node, every unavailable premise is recorded as a dependency, including a pending
strongly connected component. If all premises are present, propagation,
certification, or reduction is enabled. Core progress supplies the step for a
completed elimination. A budget suspension retains the enabled work in the
residual. These cases exhaust the graph's node forms.
\end{proof}
The residual alternative specifies the meaning of partial evaluation. The core
theorem supplies the stronger elimination guarantee when those recorded
premises are available.

\subsection{Composition and successful execution}
At a world $w$, a closure is \emph{failure-free on $A$} when its complete
executions on $A$-packets never reach $\Fail$; divergence is allowed. It is
\emph{successful on $A$} when every such execution terminates in a value.
These properties include the validation and adapter operations of its specified
call protocol.

\begin{theorem}[Open compositionality]\label{thm:composition}
Suppose $f$ and $g$ have core and protocol certificates at $A\to B$ and
$C\to D$ over $S;\Delta;\Gamma$, with $S;\Delta\vdash B\preceq C$.
Then $h=\lambda x.g(f(x))$ has a core certificate at $A\to D$.
For every $S'\sqsubseteq S$, $w\in S'$, admissible rigid valuation, and certified
linking substitution, failure-freedom of $f$ on $\sem A$ and of $g$ on
$\sem C$ implies failure-freedom of $h$ on $\sem A$. Success of both components
on those domains implies success of $h$, with result in $\sem D$.
These claims are preserved by every well-formed type substitution, including
impredicative instances.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under $x:A$, application gives $f(x):\Comp(B)$, subsumption gives
$\Comp(C)$, and another application gives $g(f(x)):\Comp(D)$.
Abstraction establishes the composite type. Persistence and certified
substitution transport the proof to the chosen store, world, and linking.

Strict execution first runs $f(x)$. A diverging first computation admits no
later failure. A successful return $y$ satisfies $\sem B\subseteq\sem C$
by soundness, so the premise on $g$ applies. For successful components the two
finite executions concatenate and the result satisfies $\sem D$. Type
substitution preserves the proof and its erased execution.
\end{proof}

A further caller-side packet or result constraint contributes a validation
demand. The corresponding failure-free claim includes that demand in its
premises. A stronger published interface similarly contributes an additional
attachment goal and observation root; the original theorem retains its original
target.

\subsection{Coherence and monotone observations}
Proof erasure preserves the source implementation, its captures, protocol,
defaults, and assertions. The exact source graph persists independently of
whether proof objects are retained in executable code.

\begin{theorem}[Erasure and declaration coherence]\label{thm:erasure}
At a fixed admitted world, grounded certificates for the same implementation
origin and source graph give the same erased executions. Reassociation,
permutation, duplication, and common-domain result splitting of interface
conjuncts preserve the exact predicate, body, and observation questions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Evaluation acts on the same erased term with the same retained assertions and
packet operations. Conjunction is associative, commutative, and idempotent;
result splitting is Proposition~\ref{prop:arrows}. The common source origin
identifies the implementation, and Proposition~\ref{prop:rc-coherence}
identifies the observation questions. Proof availability determines which work
is enabled while preserving the successful-execution predicate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Monotone certified refinement]\label{cor:refinement}
Normalization by a well-evidenced evaluator followed by source conjunction can
only restrict completed observations on the retained interface. Semantic
contradictions persist. A singleton observation can remain unchanged or become
inconsistent. Checked rejections persist at their retained scopes; pending
obligations may acquire evidence.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
State preservation gives equality for normalization and inclusion for source
conjunction. Theorem~\ref{thm:monotonicity} transports inclusion through the
fixed projections and quantifiers. Subsets of the empty set remain empty, and
subsets of a singleton contain no different atom. Rejection premises persist
by Lemma~\ref{lem:persistence}.
\end{proof}

\section{Surface language and calling protocols}
\label{sec:surface}
The surface constructs elaborate into the predicates, proof obligations, and
packet operations already defined. Function syntax writes
\code{func(parameters) -> T: e} for an implementation and
\code{func(parameters) -> T} for an interface. The result annotation creates a
body obligation. The implementation's protocol identifies its slots and their
permitted bindings.

\subsection{Universal declarations and instances}
The form \code{forall (A, B) T} binds semantic type variables in $T$;
\code{A: U} specifies an upper bound. A single unbounded variable may omit
parentheses. Quantifiers have low precedence, with parentheses delimiting
quantified arguments and results. An outer universal has the equivalent forms
\begin{lstlisting}
map(A, B): func(func(A) -> B, [...A]) -> [...B]
map: forall (A, B) func(func(A) -> B, [...A]) -> [...B]
map: func<A, B>(func(A) -> B, [...A]) -> [...B]
\end{lstlisting}
Each describes one subject at every instance. A lexical prefix inside a record
abbreviates a quantifier around that record:
\begin{lstlisting}
r: {
    forall A
    id: func(A) -> A
}
r: forall A {id: func(A) -> A}
\end{lstlisting}
The binder's private scope is retained
when another file contributes constraints to the record.

A parametric alias \code{Box(A) = {value: A}} constructs a type expression by
substitution. A quantified field \code{box(A): {value: A}} constrains one
subject by every instance. Ordinary \code{let} names an expression.
Instantiation \code{f[T]} records \textsc{All-E} on the same subject,
retaining its other constraints and its instance expression. It erases to
\code{f}. Contextual binder forms are selected by the language version, and
elaboration preserves scope, source roots, and closure origins through copying
and export.

\subsection{Arguments, omission, and partial application}
The parameter forms use the packet syntax of proposal 4484~\cite{cueproposal}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Form & Binding \\\midrule
\code{a: T} & Position or label \code{a} \\
\code{a!: T} & Required label \code{a} \\
\code{a?: T} & Optional label \code{a}; omission is absence \\
\code{_~x: T} & Position only; \code{x} is a body alias \\
\code{T} or \code{_: T} & Anonymous positional slot \\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Positional-only slots precede named slots, which precede name-only slots.
Labels identify original slots. Duplicate bindings are malformed even when
the supplied values agree. Hidden labels include package identity, and
attributes remain attached metadata.

An omission default \code{= d} is an executable part of the protocol, with a
checking obligation in its declaration environment. It supplies an argument
when the slot is omitted. An explicitly supplied description, including
\code{_}, suppresses the omission default and retains its own CUE preferences.
A required-label slot with a default is omittable because the protocol supplies
its value. An optional slot represents absence when omitted.

A packet interface includes all accepted bindings and omissions. Attachment
checks inclusion of these packet sets contravariantly. An adapter that changes
a label or adds a default is an explicit new closure. An open interface such as
\code{func(a: int, ...) -> number} contains an ordinary completion variable for
the remaining slots. Linking an implementation supplies its row, and invocation
uses the resulting protocol evidence.

A call ending in \code{...} supplies some slots and returns a closure over the
remainder. Supplied arguments are checked immediately; the body runs when the
packet is complete. Normalize partial bindings to original slot identities,
retaining unbound defaults. If $b$ is a fixed binding and $q$ a residual packet,
let $b\oplus q$ be their valid combined packet and put
$A_b=\{q\mid b\oplus q\in A\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Partial application]\label{prop:partial}
If $f:A\to B$ and $f_b(q)$ executes $f(b\oplus q)$, then $f_b:A_b\to B$.
Slot substitution transports the protocol certificate. Successive binding of
disjoint slots and simultaneous binding give the same normalized closure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A successful residual call is an original call with packet in $A$, so its result
belongs to $B$. Slot normalization records the same map from original slots
to supplied values in either grouping.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Foreign calls and finite traversal}
A foreign bridge supplies a certified closure whose public interface describes
the checked adapter. The adapter retains representation checks, conversions,
and output validation. An arbitrary-precision integer interface may consequently
have native range failures while preserving its successful-result contract.
Logical kinds are checked at attachment independently of native widths.
Erased type evidence leaves the adapter's conversion path intact~\cite{cueaddendum}.

A finite-list fold is specified by
\[
 \mathsf{fold}(s,z,[])=z,\qquad
 \mathsf{fold}(s,z,x::xs)=\mathsf{fold}(s,s(z,x),xs).
\]
Its certificate is
$\forall A,B.\,[[B,A]\to B,\,B,\,\List(A)]\to B$.
The equations define traversal by finite list length, with callback evidence
supplied at each step. User-defined calls retain the base cycle discipline,
and all higher-order evaluation is subject to accounted work~\cite{cuecycles}.

\section{Examples}
\label{sec:examples}
Each annotated declaration or call is a conformance check in its indicated
scope. Negative cases are checked separately from successful ones. Utility
definitions are reused only after they are displayed. Repeated declarations shown as
constraints on one subject are unified. Commented future refinements are
separate extensions of the initial configuration.

Evaluation results denote summaries with their source dependencies retained.
The comments distinguish constraint failure, rejected certification, and pending
work. Bodyless interfaces are hypotheses until their implementations are linked.

forces \code{empty} to be the empty list and refutes \code{impossible} and

used by \code{one}.

\subsection{Higher-order and impredicative use}
The same quantifier rules apply inside callback parameters, returned interfaces, and type arguments.

\example{A polymorphic map}\label{ex:case18}
\begin{lstlisting}
map(A, B): func(g: func(A) -> B, xs: [...A]) -> [...B]: [
    for x in xs {g(x)}
]
inc:  func(x: int) -> int: x + 1
text: func(x: int) -> string: "\(x)"
numbers: map(inc, [1, 2, 3])     // [2, 3, 4]
strings: map(text, [1, 2, 3])    // ["1", "2", "3"]
invalid: map[int, string](inc, [1]) // rejected: callback result
\end{lstlisting}
With $A,B$ rigid, each list element has type $A$ and each successful callback
return has type $B$. The result is therefore a list of $B$. The final call
requires an integer-to-string callback; \code{inc} cannot establish that result
contract.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Filtering and nested traversal}\label{ex:case19}
\begin{lstlisting}
filter(A): func(p: func(A) -> bool, xs: [...A]) -> [...A]: [
    for x in xs if p(x) {x}
]
partition(A): func(p: func(A) -> bool, xs: [...A]) -> {
    yes: [...A], no: [...A]
}: {
    yes: [for x in xs if p(x) {x}]
    no:  [for x in xs if !p(x) {x}]
}
positive: func(x: int) -> bool: x > 0
kept: filter(positive, [-2, 0, 4]) // [4]
parts: partition(positive, [-2, 0, 4]) // yes: [4], no: [-2, 0]
flatMap(A, B): func(g: func(A) -> [...B], xs: [...A]) -> [...B]: [
    for x in xs for y in g(x) {y}
]
duplicate(A): func(x: A) -> [A, A]: [x, x]
twice: flatMap[int, int](duplicate[int], [1, 2]) // [1, 1, 2, 2]
\end{lstlisting}
Filtering and partitioning select existing elements and preserve their arbitrary
type. The two partition comprehensions use a pure predicate. In \code{flatMap},
each outer call produces a list of $B$, whose elements populate the final list.
The explicit instance of \code{duplicate} supplies the required callback.
\end{minipage}\par

\subsection{Live descriptions and accumulating evidence}
Open interfaces use proved bounds while retaining the source references that future unification may constrain.

\example{A live input and a fixed alias}\label{ex:case1}
\begin{lstlisting}
let Fixed = int
fixed: func(x: Fixed) -> int: 2 * x

Live: int
live: func(x: Live) -> int: 2 * x
a: fixed(2)                 // 4
b: live(2)                  // 4

// A later file may add Live: >=0.
\end{lstlisting}
Both body proofs use the upper bound $\Int$. The alias has a fixed denotation;
the field retains a live description coordinate. Its call uses a fresh packet
and keeps the reference to \code{Live}, so further constraints on that field
continue to validate the invocation.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Input and result constraints on one call}\label{ex:case2}
\begin{lstlisting}
_input: int
_output: int
_f: func(x: _input) -> int: 2 * x
a: _f(2)
a: _output                  // 4, with both dependencies retained

// Separate possible refinements:
// _input: >=0               // preserves a: 4
// _input: >2                // _|_ at the invocation
// _output: >=4              // preserves a: 4
// _output: <4               // _|_ at the result
\end{lstlisting}
Each commented addition is a separate refinement. The input restriction remains
attached to the activation, and the output restriction concerns that call's
result cell. A compatible addition preserves $4$; an incompatible one refutes
the corresponding demand. The reusable implementation retains its integer
result contract.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{A stronger bound completes a proof}\label{ex:case3}
\begin{lstlisting}
A: number
f: func(x: A) -> int: x      // body goal pending

// A later file adds:
A: int                      // body goal can now be proved
answer: f(2)                // 2
\end{lstlisting}
The initial domain has only a numeric upper bound. A fractional point in that
bound is not a witness in every live domain. The body obligation remains
pending until the added integer bound proves $A\preceq\Int$. A fixed
\code{number} domain would instead admit the persistent counterexample $1.5$.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Proving a live result}\label{ex:case4}
\begin{lstlisting}
R: int
same: func(x: R) -> R: x                  // certified
constant: func(_: int) -> R: 2            // result goal pending
checked: func(x: int) -> R: x & R         // certified partial body

// Refining R: 2 proves the constant's result goal.
// Refining R: >2 refutes that constant result.
\end{lstlisting}
The variable return supplies $R$ directly. Proving the constant result requires
$\{2\}\preceq R$, which its upper bound alone does not establish. The assertion
in \code{checked} establishes membership on successful execution. Refinement
to the singleton supplies the constant's proof; a predicate excluding $2$
supplies its countercertificate.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Evaluation produces certification premises}\label{ex:case5}
\begin{lstlisting}
seed: func(x: int) -> int: x + 1
k: seed(2)
shift: func(x: int) -> int: x + k
limit: shift(1)
bounded: func(x: int) -> int: x & <=limit
a: bounded(3)                           // 3; limit is 4
\end{lstlisting}
The certificate for \code{seed} supplies a result bound for \code{k}. That
bound supports the body of \code{shift}, whose call computes \code{limit}.
The last assertion then finishes its data check. Bounds can precede concrete
values, but each retains the invocation evidence and validation demands
supporting it.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{A partially known capture}\label{ex:case6}
\begin{lstlisting}
factor: int
scale: func(x: int) -> int: factor * x  // certified with a live capture
factor: 3
answer: scale(4)                       // 12

_f: {
    z!: int
    f: func(x: int, y: int) -> int: x + y + z
}
bar: (_f & {z: 10}).f(100, 1000)        // 1110
\end{lstlisting}
The multiplication proof uses the integer bound on \code{factor}. Completing
the multiplication also needs its value. Once that value is supplied the result
is $12$, with the capture dependency retained. A later contradiction in the
capture therefore remains a contradiction of the demanded computation.
The required capture in \code{_f} likewise supplies a body-proof premise before
it supplies a runtime value. The meet completes a fresh instance whose method
sees \code{z: 10}; it does not fill the prototype. A call through the unrefined
prototype remains incomplete. Separate instances retain independent captures.
Source export may share their code origin, but must instantiate each live
capture environment in its lexical record so later copying and refinement
continue to rebind it.
\end{minipage}\par

\end{lstlisting}

// Separate possible refinements:

of its partial arrow.

\subsection{Conjunctive and relevant interfaces}
An intersection constrains one implementation. Its explicit observations determine the relevant admission questions.

\example{A shared quantified record}\label{ex:case25}
\begin{lstlisting}

    id:   func(x: A) -> A: x
    wrap: func(x: A) -> {value: A}: {value: x}
}

wrapped: utilities.wrap[string]("payload") // {value: "payload"}

// interface.cue
wrap(A): func(A) -> {value: A}
// metadata.cue
wrap(B): func(B) -> {tag: "wrapped"}
// implementation.cue
wrap(T): func(x: T) -> {value: T, tag: "wrapped"}: {
    value: x, tag: "wrapped"
}
\end{lstlisting}
The fields of \code{utilities} retain the record's common witness when selected.
The separate \code{wrap} declarations align by alpha-renaming. One implementation
then supplies both the value field and the metadata field at each instance,
using the conjunction law for common-domain results.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Contravariant attachment}\label{ex:case26}

bad: apply(ints)                         // rejected at linking
\end{lstlisting}
The first attachment requires $\Num\preceq\Int$, which the fractional packet
$1.5$ refutes. The second requires the valid inclusion $\Int\preceq\Num$.
The higher-order call makes the same coverage check at linking, before its
client uses the promised numeric capability.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Regions of a conjunctive interface}\label{ex:case27}

The two \code{convert} clauses constrain disjoint input regions of one closure.
The clauses of \code{refine} both apply on integers. The body tests of

code. The interface conjunction preserves both obligations without choosing
an implementation.
\end{minipage}\par

\subsection{Calling protocols and implementation identity}
Packet binding describes actual calling behavior, and closure equality preserves the identity of the implementation being constrained.

\example{Slot binding and omission}\label{ex:case35}
\begin{lstlisting}
sum: func(a: int, b: int) -> int: a + b
p: sum(2, 3)                  // 5
q: sum(a: 2, b: 3)            // 5
r: sum(2, b: 3)               // 5
key: func(a!: int, note?: string) -> int: a
k: key(a: 4)                  // 4; note absent
n: key(a: 4, note: "audit")   // 4
badKey: key(4)                // rejected: a is name-only
scale: func(_~value: int, _: int) -> int: value * 2
s: scale(5, 99)               // 10
badLabel: scale(value: 5, 99)  // rejected: label and order
bad1: sum(1, a: 1)            // duplicate binding
bad2: sum(1, 2, 3)            // surplus argument
bad3: sum(a: 1, b: 2, c: 3)   // unknown label
bad4: sum(1)                  // missing argument
pending: sum(_, 2)            // supplied but incomplete
\end{lstlisting}
Position and label resolve to original slots before value constraints are
checked. Duplicate bindings therefore remain invalid even when equal.
A body-only alias introduces no callable label. Optional fields require
presence evidence before selection; a supplied unknown operand is a present
slot whose value is still demanded.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Package-qualified labels}\label{ex:case36}
\begin{lstlisting}
package internal
measure: func(_value: int @go(Int), unit!: string) -> int: _value
inside: measure(_value: 7, unit: "ms")
// Other packages can supply the positional slot, not this hidden label.
\end{lstlisting}
The hidden label carries its declaring package identity. Inside that package
it can be supplied by name; the positional slot remains available externally.
The attribute belongs to the descriptor as metadata and leaves ordinary binding
unchanged.
\end{minipage}\par

\par\addvspace{6pt}\noindent
\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Defaults belong to the protocol}\label{ex:case37}
\begin{lstlisting}
add: func(a: int, b: int = 10) -> int: a + b
x: int | *5
omitted:  add(1)             // 11
supplied: add(1, x)          // 6
unknown:  add(1, _)          // incomplete
render: func(text: string, suffix!: string = "!") -> string:
    text + suffix
hello: render("hello")                 // "hello!"
question: render("hello", suffix: "?") // "hello?"
badDefault: func(x: int = "x") -> int: x // rejected
\end{lstlisting}
Omission supplies the declared default. A supplied argument retains its own
preference and suppresses that default, even while incomplete. The required-label
slot in \code{render} is omittable because its protocol supplies a value.
The final default conflicts with its parameter constraint at declaration.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Adapters are implementations}\label{ex:case38}
\begin{lstlisting}

named: func(x: int) -> int: raw(x)
configured: func(x: int = 2) -> int: raw(x)
a: named(x: 2)              // 3
b: configured()            // 3
\end{lstlisting}
The wrappers introduce the stated label and omission behavior as executable
protocols. Each has its own origin and a body calling \code{raw}. Their

retains its original positional slot.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Open rows and normalized partial bindings}\label{ex:case39}
\begin{lstlisting}
T: func(a: int, ...) -> number
f: T & (func(a: int, b: int) -> int: a + b)
x: f(1, 2)                   // 3
missing: f(1)                // rejected: b is required
add3: func(a: int, b: int, c: int) -> int: a + b + c

part: func(int, int) -> int
last: part(2, ...)

same: add3(a: 1, ...) & part
again: same(2, 3)            // 6
\end{lstlisting}
Linking supplies the open interface's remaining slot, including its requiredness.
Each partial application retains the unbound requirements and any defaults.
Binding by the original label or by its position yields the same normalized
slot map, so the two occurrences constrained by \code{same} identify the
same partial closure.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Closure identity through copying}\label{ex:case40}

\example{Shared results and implementation alternatives}\label{ex:case41}

The body of \code{both} explicitly combines its two result descriptions and

implementations; preference selection chooses an alternative according to the
base default rules, with its source dependencies preserved.

\subsection{Validation, boundaries, and finite computation}
Validation demands and primitive certificates connect the type discipline to completed observations.

\example{Validation across field and call boundaries}\label{ex:case42}
\begin{lstlisting}
positive: func(x: int) -> bool: x > 0
#Positive: {value: int, _valid: true & positive(value)}
n: (#Positive & {value: 7}).value  // 7
bad: #Positive & {value: -1}       // _|_
raw: func(x: number) -> string: "\(x)"
call: {arg: number, result: raw(arg)}
call: {arg: int, result: string}   // another file's restriction

wrong: (call & {arg: 1.5}).result // _|_
\end{lstlisting}
The validator's hidden dependency survives selection of \code{value}.
The two declarations of \code{call} constrain one existential packet and result
while the reusable numeric implementation keeps its capability. The integer
restriction excludes the fractional invocation without changing that
implementation.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Conditional certificates and native checks}\label{ex:case43}
\begin{lstlisting}
legacyBody: func(int) -> int
checked: func(x: int) -> (int & >0): legacyBody(x) & >0
externF: func(int) -> int       // supplied by a checked native adapter
small: externF(42)
large: externF(1000000000000000000000000000000)
fractional: externF(1.5)        // rejected: logical argument mismatch
\end{lstlisting}
Bodyless function fields supply named hypotheses for client checking; actual
implementations discharge them at linking. The explicit assertion retains the
positive-result check. A native adapter may reject the large integer at its
representation boundary while preserving its portable partial contract.
The fractional argument conflicts with the logical integer input.
\end{minipage}\par

\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}
\example{Finite iteration and repeated invocation}\label{ex:case44}
\begin{lstlisting}
fold(A, B): func(step: func(B, A) -> B, seed: B, xs: [...A]) -> B
plus: func(x: int, y: int) -> int: x + y
sum: fold[int, int](plus, 0, [1, 2, 3]) // 6
empty: fold[int, int](plus, 0, [])      // 0
twice: func(n: int) -> int: n + n
nested: twice(twice(twice(2)))          // 16
\end{lstlisting}
The \code{fold} hypothesis is linked to the finite-list primitive. Its empty
case returns the seed, and each nonempty case consumes one list cell after a
certified step. Length induction supplies the universal certificate. Repeated
nested calls create ordinary finite activations.
\end{minipage}\par

\example{Direct contradictions and residual equations}\label{ex:case45}

emptyData: {a: int} & {a: bool} // _|_

\end{lstlisting}
The integer interval and the incompatible required field are mandatory

a stronger checked arithmetic proof can refute it directly. The ground
constraint exposes the inconsistency to elementary propagation. An unresolved
residual continues to carry those equations.

\section{Related designs}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{CUE function signatures}
Proposal 4484 and its theory addendum are compared at repository revision
\code{a949162aedb8}, dated 19 September 2026~\cite{cueproposal,cueaddendum}.
The addendum interprets signatures as call filters: input constraints meet
covariantly and calls outside them fail. It identifies modular checkability as
the resulting loss. Its proposed coverage component joins required domains
while call restrictions meet, and checks an inclusion between the two. It also
identifies result-side attachment checking as a separate obligation.

These observations isolate the distinction between a constraint on a call and
a capability required from an implementation. A client expecting
$\Num\to\Str$ can use the packet $1.5$. An integer-only implementation fails
the coverage goal $\Num\preceq\Int$, even if every integer call succeeds.
Covariant tightening can retain a coherent call filter while accepting that
attachment and postponing its failure to invocation. The present attachment
judgment makes the capability obligation explicit. Call-local restrictions
remain ordinary predicates on existential packet and result cells.

The addendum's pointwise body conjunction constructs behavior by combining
results. The main proposal describes opaque function identity; the two accounts
are distinct~\cite{cueproposal,cueaddendum}. Here conjunction intersects
predicates on one closure, and application satisfies the inclusion in
Proposition~\ref{prop:app}. Explicit adapters provide new protocols or combine
result descriptions. A module replacement judgment obtains its orientation
from the clients to be preserved, whereas conjunction retains its symmetric
logical meaning.

\subsection{Set-theoretic types and partial behavior}
Semantic subtyping interprets Boolean type structure and arrow intersections
extensionally, retaining correlations between inputs and
results~\cite{semantic}. Its polymorphic developments combine instantiation,
subtyping, and expansion under stated semantic assumptions~\cite{castagnaxu,poly1,poly2}.
CDuce's polymorphism tutorial illustrates intersections obtained from several
instances of a higher-order map~\cite{cduce}. Exact observation regions have the
same correlation-preserving structure. Polymorphic inference for dynamic
languages provides another development of precise Boolean interfaces~\cite{dynamic}.

The semantic arrow and the operational evidence interface determine which of
those checking rules can be transported here. In particular, a partial arrow
with an empty result may have inhabitants. Relevant admission concerns its
explicit observations, and invocation evidence concerns its usable protocol.
Each imported inference rule must preserve those interpretations and transport
the evidence required by eliminations.

Elixir's strong-arrow account additionally reasons about failure outside a
declared input domain, using existing guards and operations at dynamic
boundaries~\cite{elixirstrong}. A successful-return contract and an
out-of-domain failure property express different facts. This proposal's arrow
states the former; its explicit assertions, source packet constraints, and
native adapters retain the checks that determine actual failure.

\subsection{Impredicativity and proof-directed checking}
Intersection interpretations of erased universals have precedents in
realizability semantics of System~F~\cite{pistone}. Their inhabitants are fixed
operational realizers, and arrows are predicates on those realizers. Full
set-function spaces $B^A$ give a different interpretation, to which Reynolds's
set-theoretic obstruction applies~\cite{reynolds}. In the present model the
powerset carrier is fixed before type interpretation. Unary predicates already
force partial identity at singleton instances; relational parametricity concerns
relations between interpretations at different instances.

Bidirectional typing makes synthesis and checking obligations
explicit~\cite{bidirectional}. Predicative higher-rank checking has a complete
bidirectional account~\cite{dunfield}, and higher-rank systems with intersection
and union types supply further algorithmic precedents~\cite{jiang}.
Value-restricted union elimination preserves a shared evaluated
subject~\cite{unions}. Here an impredicative instance is a finite proof input,
and open checking additionally records the store facts supporting its
inclusions. Search produces checked evidence within an accounted run.

\subsection{Predicate logic and information propagation}
The adjunctions in Proposition~\ref{prop:adjunction} are the logical structure
identified by Lawvere; Melli\`es and Zeilberger develop a bifibrational account
of the corresponding presheaf construction~\cite{lawvere,mellies}. They also
explain composition of open descriptions. For $R\subseteq X\times Y$ and
$S\subseteq Y\times Z$,
\[
 (S\circ R)(x,z)\iff\exists y.\ R(x,y)\land S(y,z).
\]
Equality is the identity relation, and associativity follows by exchanging
existential witnesses. In the category of sets and relations, Cartesian
juxtaposition is a tensor and equality supplies self-duality: its snake equation
is $\exists y,z.\ (x=y)\land(y=z)\land(z=w)\iff x=w$.
These equations also hold in a definable subcategory closed under the stated
operations. Conjunction is the meet in one predicate fiber; juxtaposition
combines interfaces.

Propagators accumulate partial information in cells and notify their
dependents~\cite{propagators}. Abstract interpretation organizes sound summaries
and their fixed-point computation by an information order~\cite{abstract}.
The current bounds are summaries of a retained scoped relation, and proof facts
are another form of accumulated information. Finite support justifies each
consequence, while exact residualization preserves the underlying problem
through incomplete search and subsequent source unification.

\section{Conclusion}
Universal quantification extends the completion interpretation of CUE by
constraining one subject at every semantic type instance. On operational
closures it yields partial function contracts with contravariant inputs,
covariant results, and correlation-preserving intersections.

Open certification proves those contracts relative to a store. Live references
retain their meanings while their established bounds become more precise.
Evaluation and certification consequently share a proof-producing propagation
process, with exact residuals preserving every required dependency. Relevant
observations distinguish interface admission from semantic membership, and
positive refutation evidence gives stable diagnostics. Soundness, operational
safety, and store-relative composition then describe the guarantees of each
certified configuration and of its further refinements.

\section{CUE integration requirements}
\label{app:criteria}
The semantic and integration requirements in Section~9 of the theory addendum
have the following realizations~\cite{cueaddendum}.

\paragraph{Directionality.}
Contracts on implementations and restrictions on individual calls are both
predicates combined by intersection. Attachment explicitly identifies the
required interface and the supplied implementation, giving the inclusion
$A\preceq C$ its orientation. This judgment applies to provided functions,
callbacks, and separately linked code without guessing a module-level role.
Live inclusions retain their proof obligations.

\paragraph{Stable observations under added constraints.}
The exact residual invariant preserves the predicate on the full exposed
interface. New source constraints intersect that predicate. Corollary~\ref{cor:refinement}
therefore preserves contradiction and permits an atomic result only to stay the
same or become inconsistent. Positive refutation evidence persists in its scope.
Default presentation and comprehension dependencies remain governed by the base
observation policy and are retained for source refinement.

\paragraph{Input and output invariants across files.}
A call has existential packet cells and a shared result. Any file may constrain
those cells, independently of the reusable implementation contract.
Example~\ref{ex:case2} constrains both sides of one call, and
Example~\ref{ex:case42} gives a reusable call record. Additional implementation
requirements create attachment goals with the same scope and dependency rules.

\paragraph{Callable self-unification.}
A complete implementation denotes a singleton, whose intersection with itself
is unchanged. Its identity uses origin, free captures, and normalized partial
bindings. The same closure reached through copying or embedding consequently
retains its invocation evidence without a pointer-identity test. Unrelated
record refinements preserve that identity. Pending capture equality remains a
scoped constraint; Example~\ref{ex:case40} exhibits both equal and unequal cases.

\paragraph{Portable foreign interfaces.}
The public contract describes the checked adapter. Representation limits remain
executable boundary checks with ordinary failure outcomes, so portable logical
signatures can remain independent of native widths. The existing conversion
path is preserved by proof erasure. Logical input and result obligations are
certified separately from representation checks, as in
Example~\ref{ex:case43}.

\section{Auxiliary results}
\label{app:auxiliary}
The following lemmas make explicit the remaining local arguments used by the
surface elaboration and evaluator interface.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-list traversal]\label{prop:fold}
Suppose $s$ is certified at $[B,A]\to B$, the seed $b$ at $B$, and a finite
list at $\List(A)$ in one store and scope. The left fold has result certificate
$B$. It is failure-free when every encountered step is failure-free and
successful when every encountered step is successful.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the list length. The empty case returns the certified seed. The
nonempty case first calls $s$ on the accumulator and head. A successful result
has type $B$, so the induction hypothesis applies to the tail. Failure
propagates, divergence prevents later failure, and successful finite steps
concatenate. Certificate substitution and store weakening transport the
argument to every admissible instance and refinement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Attachment preserves an inhabitant]\label{prop:attach-world}
At a completed world, $\{f\}\cap\sem T$ is either $\{f\}$ or
$\varnothing$. Grounded certified attachment gives the first case and preserves
the closure's erased execution. Symbolic attachment retains its live dependencies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A singleton has only these two subsets. Certificate soundness establishes
membership, and erasure retains the code and captures. The residual invariant
preserves the source constraints independently of the executable proof object.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Contextual residual equivalence]\label{prop:future}
Equal graph predicates on a common exposed interface give equal semantic observations
after any common finite sequence of source conjunctions and semantic projections.
The property is preserved by a common fresh context extension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conjoining the same predicate preserves equality. Existential projection,
universal projection, and inverse image are functions on predicates and
preserve equality. Induct on the sequence; Proposition~\ref{prop:adjunction}
handles reindexing along the context extension. A coordinate remains exposed
until all source constraints addressing it have been applied.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Grounding a dependency cycle]\label{prop:no-cycle}
A finite set of goals whose proposed derivations each require another goal in
the set supplies no grounded certificate by those dependencies alone. Grounding
requires independent evidence or a checked inference discharging the relevant
hypotheses.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Follow a required dependency of a purported grounded root within the set.
Without an independent leaf or discharge rule, this path stays in the finite
set and repeats a goal. It would therefore require a cyclic proof graph,
contrary to well-foundedness. A checked hypothesis-discharge inference supplies
a finite derivation rather than such a dependency cycle.
\end{proof}
